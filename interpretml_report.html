Black-box explanations for a frozen AI text detector

A traceable account of the numeric inputs given to InterpretML, the outputs it generated, and what those outputs do and do not support.

Executive summary

Purpose, observed result and scope of the experiment.

Outcome: the experiment successfully demonstrated black-box explainability for the detector’s eight numeric stylometric inputs. The model was evaluated on a class-balanced sample of 10,000 labeled RAID records, then explained with Morris sensitivity, partial dependence, LIME and Kernel SHAP. Measured accuracy was 60.99%; the strongest global Morris effect was average word length. Explanations describe how this frozen model responds to numeric feature vectors; they do not explain individual words or establish authorship.

7 · Decision-making analysis

What a reviewer can learn from the explanations without overreading them.

Observed local patterns

  • For the correctly classified AI example, P(AI)=0.90; average word length was the largest positive local score in both LIME and Kernel SHAP.
  • For the human example misclassified as AI, P(AI)=0.62. Kernel SHAP’s largest positive score was average word length (+0.1447), while punctuation count was a negative score (−0.0737).
  • For the AI example misclassified as human, P(AI)=0.00. Both methods placed average word length, average sentence length, and word count among the strongest negative scores.
  • In the what-if runs, changing only average word length flipped labels in multiple cases; the largest recorded P(AI) movement was 0.90 to 0.07 for Correct AI.

What human reviewers can use

  • See which numeric cues align with a particular model output and where the model is sensitive.
  • Compare a correct and incorrect example to identify behavior worth further validation.
  • Use explanations to guide dataset audits or robustness checks, not to adjudicate an individual author.
  • Notice high observed feature outliers and check whether they represent plausible text statistics.
LIME and SHAP scores approximate the detector locally. Global Morris/PDP outputs depend on the sample. Feature contributions can be correlated and are not causes; the explanation does not reach inside the prose or establish why a person wrote it.

8 · Limitations and conclusion

InterpretML successfully explained the frozen predictor’s numeric interface for this experiment; scope remains limited to the evidence recorded here.

Limitations

  • The model uses eight surface-level stylometric statistics and may not generalize across domains, generators, attacks or time.
  • RAID is separate from the model card’s stated training source, but source overlap cannot be fully excluded from available IDs.
  • LIME and Kernel SHAP are approximations whose results can depend on the background and perturbation choices; repeat checks only measure stability for these cases/settings.
  • Partial dependence can evaluate unrealistic feature combinations; feature-space what-if vectors may not correspond to valid text.
  • Selected local cases are illustrative examples from the evaluation sample, not a representative explanation census.
  • A detector score is not proof of authorship and must not be used for individual accusations.

Conclusion

The project supplied a verified, frozen 100-tree Random Forest with a deterministic eight-feature interface to InterpretML. InterpretML generated Morris and partial-dependence global summaries plus LIME and Kernel SHAP local explanations for four real evaluation cases. The measured run had 60.99% accuracy on 10,000 balanced RAID records. The most prominent global pattern in the saved Morris results was average word length; local cases also showed that this feature could strongly move model output in either direction. This demonstrates black-box explainability for numeric model inputs, not raw-text understanding, causality, or reliable human authorship detection.
